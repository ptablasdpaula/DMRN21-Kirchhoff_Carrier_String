\begin{abstract}
We present a differentiable FDTD Kirchhoff--Carrier string whose
backpropagation runs in a custom CUDA kernel that stores only periodic
checkpoints and three scalars per time step. A fourth-order spatial stencil
removes grid dispersion, allowing lower internal oversampling. Peak memory
stays nearly flat with batch size, and gradients are faster and far leaner
than with a state-of-the-art differentiable modal solver.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Although FDTD synthesis is increasingly leaner~\cite{bilbao2024guitar},
differentiable frameworks~\cite{engel2020ddsp} largely keep away from it,
since backpropagating through a time-stepping scheme results in deep
recurrent differentiation graphs, expensive in time and memory. Instead, the
literature has favoured cheaper approaches like digital
waveguides~\cite{tablas2026waveguides,tablas2026sound}, which do not
naturally capture nonlinearities and collisions, and modal
synthesis~\cite{diaz2025}. In this work we demonstrate that an FDTD string
can be backpropagated faster, and in far less memory, than a current
differentiable modal solver.

\section{Model and method}
The string is the damped Kirchhoff--Carrier stiff string, simply supported at
both ends:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\rho A\frac{\partial^2u}{\partial t^2}={}&T_0\frac{\partial^2u}{\partial x^2}
 -EI\frac{\partial^4u}{\partial x^4}
 -2\rho A\sigma_0\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}\\
 &+2\rho A\sigma_1\frac{\partial^3u}{\partial t\,\partial x^2}
 +\frac{EA}{2L}\!\left(\int_0^L\Big(\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}\Big)^2dx\right)
 \frac{\partial^2u}{\partial x^2},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:pde}
\end{equation}
with density $\rho$, cross-section $A$, Young's modulus $E$, second moment of
area $I$, tension $T_0$ and damping $\sigma_0,\sigma_1$; the last term couples
every mode through one integral.

\head{Time stepping}
Russo et al.~\cite{russo2026} integrate (\ref{eq:pde}) with the conservative
Kirchhoff--Carrier scheme of~\cite[Sec.~8.1.2]{bilbao2009}, extended with
stiffness and loss terms. With $u^{n}$ the grid vector at time $nk$, grid
spacing $h$ and $D_{xx},D_{xxxx}$ the difference matrices,
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
\mathbf{A}u^{n+1}=\mathbf{B}u^{n}+\mathbf{C}u^{n-1}-q\,\alpha^n,\\
q=\tfrac{k}{2}\sqrt{hEA/L}\,D_{xx}u^{n},\qquad
\alpha^n=q^{\mathsf T}\big(u^{n+1}+u^{n-1}\big),
\end{gathered}
\label{eq:update}
\end{equation}
with $\mathbf{A}=\rho A(1+\sigma_0k)I$ (mass and damping),
$\mathbf{B}=2\rho AI+(T_0k^2+2\rho A\sigma_1k)D_{xx}-EIk^2D_{xxxx}$ (current
state) and $\mathbf{C}=-\rho A(1-\sigma_0k)I-2\rho A\sigma_1kD_{xx}$ (previous
state). Up to a constant, $\alpha^n$ is the Kirchhoff--Carrier integral
averaged over the two neighbouring time levels, the choice that gives an exact
discrete energy balance~\cite[eq.~8.9]{bilbao2009}. It contains $u^{n+1}$:
the scheme is implicit only through this scalar, which Sherman--Morrison gives
in closed form:
\begin{equation}
\alpha^n=\frac{q^{\mathsf T}u^{n-1}+q^{\mathsf T}\mathbf{A}^{-1}
 (\mathbf{B}u^{n}+\mathbf{C}u^{n-1})}{1+q^{\mathsf T}\mathbf{A}^{-1}q}.
\label{eq:alpha}
\end{equation}
In the discrete sine basis $D_{xx}$, $\mathbf{B}$ and $\mathbf{C}$ are
diagonal, so with $\alpha^n$ known (\ref{eq:update}) updates each mode
independently.

\head{Cached adjoint}
Instead of every state of every step, we keep $(u^{n},u^{n-1})$ every 256 steps
and, per step, only $\alpha^n$ and the two inner products behind
(\ref{eq:alpha}), $q^{\mathsf T}q$ and
$q^{\mathsf T}(\mathbf{B}u^{n}+\mathbf{C}u^{n-1})$. On the reverse pass each
segment is re-run from its checkpoint, cheaply since with $\alpha^n$ known
(\ref{eq:update}) is diagonal, and differentiated backwards with the stored
inner products supplying the derivatives of $\alpha^n$ through
(\ref{eq:alpha}). A CUDA kernel runs this in parallel over modes and batch
items.

\head{Spatial stencil}
Spatial derivatives use the fourth-order five-point stencil with parameter
$7/6$ of~\cite[Sec.~6.3.1]{bilbao2009}, closed at the simply supported ends
by odd reflection; it is diagonal in the same sine basis, so only the mode
eigenvalues change, and on our 176-interval grid the waveform error against
a converged solution of Russo et al.'s scheme~\cite{russo2026} drops from
11.7\% to 1\% at 2$\times$ oversampling.

\section{Evaluation}
Each evaluation is one sound-matching step: a batch of strings is simulated
for 1 s, the mean absolute error of each waveform against the target's is
computed, and backpropagated to ten parameters: the eight physical ones of
(\ref{eq:pde}) plus the pluck and pickup positions. The target is a steel
string ($\rho=8050$ kg/m$^3$, $T_0=75$ N, $r=0.356$ mm, $E=174$ GPa,
$L=1$ m, $\sigma_0=0.92$ s$^{-1}$, $\sigma_1=2.9\times10^{-4}$ m$^2$/s), plucked
at its centre with a 2 mm raised cosine of half-width $L/4$ and read at its
midpoint; the batch perturbs every parameter by up to $\pm3\%$. We compare our
method with direct PyTorch autodiff of the same FDTD and a 100-mode
\texttt{jaxdiffmodal} Kirchhoff--Carrier string~\cite{diaz2025} on an A100
80 GB in float64; the FDTD uses 176 intervals at 88.2 kHz, decimated to
44.1 kHz. As seen in
Fig.~\ref{fig:benchmark}, our method is nearly three orders of magnitude
faster than plain autodiff and faster than \texttt{jaxdiffmodal}, and its
memory grows slowly with batch size, whereas both baselines grow linearly and
eventually run out.
